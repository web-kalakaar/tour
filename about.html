About K. Dixon Tour

A Beawar-based team, built on years of local research and first-hand knowledge of the Walled City.

How we started

Built by people who grew up inside the walls

K. Dixon Tour was started by a small group of Beawar residents who noticed the same thing on every trip they helped friends and relatives plan: visitors to Beawar were being handed a map, not a story. The town's four gates, its cotton-trade past, its role in the Right to Information movement — none of it was reaching the people passing through.

We began as informal weekend walks through the Parkota, the old Walled City, for visiting family. Word spread. What started as a favour became a proper agency, built around the same principle: every tour should be led by someone who has actually researched the place, not memorised a script.

Our mission

To make Beawar's history accessible and interesting to every visitor — from a family on a weekend trip to a student researching Rajasthan's colonial-era towns — through guides who treat local history as a subject worth studying, not just repeating.

What makes us different

  • Every guide personally researches the sites they walk — municipal records, oral histories, and published sources on Beawar's founding.
  • Small group sizes, so questions actually get answered instead of a rehearsed monologue.
  • Transparent, fixed pricing communicated before booking — no on-trip surprises.
  • Local transport partners who know Beawar's roads and the Aravali routes around it.

Founder & head guide

Rajendra Kanojiya

Founder, K. Dixon Tour — 16 years leading tours in Beawar and Ajmer district

Rajendra grew up near Chang Gate and began guiding informally in his twenties, walking visiting relatives through the old market lanes. Over sixteen years he built relationships with local historians, municipal record-keepers, and long-time shopkeepers in the Parkota — the same network that now informs every tour K. Dixon Tour runs.

"Most people only see Beawar from a bus window on the way to Ajmer or Pushkar. I wanted to build something that gives them a reason to stop — and actually learn what happened inside these walls."
